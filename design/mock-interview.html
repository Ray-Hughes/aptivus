The contract

  • No hints. The hint panel is closed for the whole round. You will not be offered one, so you cannot spend one by reflex.
  • No solutions. Not while it runs, and not the moment you pass. They unlock on the scorecard, for free, for both problems.
  • One clock. 45 minutes across both problems — not half each. It does not pause and it does not stop for a phone call.
  • Move freely. Switch between the two as often as you like. Your code, your scratchpad and your test results are all kept.
  • Zero is not a wall. Nothing slams shut. You roll into overtime and the scorecard counts it separately, because stopping you mid-thought teaches nothing.
  • The round pays no gems. Problems you solve clean still pay the usual 2 / 4 / 6, first solve only. Nothing is paid for pressing Start.

The clock starts on the click. Press Esc to end it at any point — nothing is scored until you do.

Why a mock pays no gems

      
Gems buy hints and solutions. A mock offers neither, so paying you for finishing one would be paying you for pressing a button — and paying you more for a round you abandoned early would be worse.

      
So the round itself is worth nothing, and the problems inside it pay exactly what they always pay: 2 / 4 / 6 for a first clean solve by difficulty, against the 30-a-day cap. Every solve in a mock is clean by definition, because there is nothing here to spend.
